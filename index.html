Read overlap by magnitude. For the two possible outputs, |overlap| rises 0.50 → 0.875 → 0.969 → 0.992 as d grows. Entanglement remains maximal; distinguishability is what collapses. The signed overlap is retained in the technical figure because the phase is part of the exact state description.

maximal entanglement is the trap; entanglement itself isn't

For unitaries, separable inputs are known to match maximally entangled ones. What fails in this flagship pair is specific: the maximally entangled probe makes the two channel outputs harder to tell apart than a plain product probe does. That is a property of this channel pair, not of entanglement as a resource.

At d = 1024 the numbers are theory only: there is no live path that executes a register of that size. At d = 256 the executable value is about 0.508, and 4000 trials give a standard error near 0.008, so that gap from the chance line is smaller than two standard errors and cannot be resolved from chance at that trial count.

The circle holds the spectrum of the channel-relating unitary. The convex hull is the set a separable probe can reach; the centroid is where the maximally entangled probe lands. The separable nearest point is attained by the flagship product-probe strategy.

A note on sources: the flagship effect is credited to Lien & Quintino (2026, preprint). The one-query discrimination bound used for the ceiling is D'Ariano, Lo Presti and Paris, J. Opt. B 4 S273 (2002). The generalized channel-discrimination formulas used for the other comparison pairs are Manna, Das Bhowmik and Saha, Phys. Rev. A 112, 042215 (2025).
